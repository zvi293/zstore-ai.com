Accessibility statement.

I want everyone to be able to use zstore-ai.com, whatever device, browser or assistive technology they rely on. This site aims to meet WCAG 2.2 Level AA. It's hand-coded with semantic HTML, works with a keyboard and respects reduced motion, and the accessibility button lets you change text, color and motion. If anything gets in your way, tell me and I'll reply within one business day.

What's built into every page?

Accessibility here starts in the code, not in the button. These are the things I build into every page of this site before it goes live.

  • Real structure

    Semantic HTML: one H1 per page, headings in order, landmarks, lists, and real buttons and links that assistive technology understands.

  • Full keyboard use

    Everything works with Tab, Enter, Space and Escape, focus is always visible, and a “Skip to content” link jumps past the navigation.

  • Readable contrast

    Body text is designed for at least 4.5 : 1 against its background (3 : 1 for large type), and color is never the only way information is shown.

  • Motion you control

    The site follows your device's reduced-motion setting, and the Motion switch in the menu stops animation everywhere, the 3D Z included.

  • Zoom and reflow

    Pages are built mobile first and reflow down to 320px wide, the equivalent of 400% zoom on a desktop screen, without sideways scrolling.

  • Described images

    Meaningful images have descriptive alt text; purely decorative shapes are hidden from screen readers so they don't add noise.

  • Clear forms

    Every field has a visible label, errors are explained in words next to the field, and nothing on the site has a time limit.

  • Well-behaved pop-ups

    Menus and panels are native dialogs: focus stays inside, Escape or your phone's Back button closes them, and focus returns where you were.

What's in the accessibility panel?

Eighteen settings in five groups (the big cursor shows only with a mouse). Open it with the round button in the navigation bar on a computer, from Accessibility in the menu on a phone or tablet, or with Alt + Shift + A (Option on a Mac).

01Text

Size, spacing and type

Text size in four steps up to 200%, more line height, wider letter and word spacing, and a plain, readable system font.

02Color

Dark, contrast, calm

Dark mode, high contrast, grayscale and low saturation.

03Reading

Find your place

Highlight links or headings, a reading guide, a reading mask, a bold focus outline, a big cursor and hide images.

04Motion

Make it still

Pause animations (the same switch as Motion in the menu) and stop anything that loops.

05Listen

Read aloud

Your device's own voice reads the main content, with pause and stop, where your browser supports it.

Your choices are saved in your browser on this device and applied before the page appears, so nothing flashes. Nothing is sent to me or anyone else. The panel adds to your own tools; browser zoom, your device's display settings and screen readers work as normal.

What are the known limitations?

No site is perfect, and an honest list helps you more than a promise. These are the gaps I know about today.

Updated as I fix them

  1. The 3D Z on the home page

    It's decorative and hidden from screen readers. It has a still fallback when WebGL isn't available, and it stops with the Motion switch.

  2. Drag-to-play demos

    Some pages have small interactive demos. Each is designed with a button or keyboard path as well; if you find one that doesn't work for you, please tell me.

  3. Words inside illustrations

    Some illustrations include a short headline inside the image. The same words are in the image's alt text.

  4. Panel text size

    It enlarges the page content; the navigation bar keeps its size so it never covers the page. To enlarge absolutely everything, use your browser's zoom.

  5. Read aloud

    It uses the voices built into your device, so whether it's available and how it sounds depends on your browser.

  6. Settings stay on one device

    They're stored in this browser only, so they don't follow you to another phone, computer or browser.

  7. Other services

    Links to outside services, such as WhatsApp or your email app, open their own pages, which have their own accessibility.

How do I report a problem?

Email or message me. I read every report myself and reply within one business day, with a fix or a clear plan. If you need something from this site in another format, ask and I'll send it.

It helps to include:

  • The page where it happened (its address or name).
  • What you were trying to do and what got in the way.
  • Your setup, if you'd like: device, browser and any assistive technology.

Ask me directly →
Do I need the accessibility panel if I already use a screen reader?

No. The site is built to work with screen readers, keyboards and your device's own settings on their own. The panel is an extra for people who want to change how the page looks or moves without changing settings on their whole device.

Are my accessibility settings stored anywhere?

Only in your own browser, on this device. Nothing is sent to me or anyone else, and Reset all in the panel clears them.

Can I get the content of this site in another format?

Yes. Email or message me with what you need, for example the text of a page as a plain document or a short call to go through it, and I'll reply within one business day.
