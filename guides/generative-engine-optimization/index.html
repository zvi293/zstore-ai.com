What is generative engine optimization (GEO)?

Generative engine optimization (GEO) is the practice of making your website easy for AI-powered search tools, like ChatGPT search, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity, to find, understand and cite when they answer questions.

It isn't a replacement for SEO: it builds on the same foundations, then adds clear, quotable answers, consistent business facts and deliberate access for AI crawlers.

How do AI search tools decide what to cite?

Most of them search first and write second: they pull pages from a search index at the moment you ask, build an answer from the most relevant and trustworthy passages, and link some of those pages as sources.

The technical name is retrieval-augmented generation. The language model doesn't answer from memory alone. It's handed fresh search results and writes from them. So the first hurdle is old-fashioned: your page has to be in the index the tool searches, and it has to contain a passage that answers the question better than the alternatives.

Google describes its AI Overviews and AI Mode as grounded in its core ranking systems, and it uses a technique it calls query fan-out: one question becomes several related searches, run in parallel, so the answer can draw on pages that match different parts of the question.

Other tools run their own crawlers and indexes. As each company documents it at the time of writing:

  • ChatGPT search uses OpenAI's OAI-SearchBot crawler plus partner data. GPTBot, the crawler that collects training data, is a separate bot.
  • Perplexity uses its own crawler, PerplexityBot.
  • Claude uses Claude-SearchBot for search. ClaudeBot is the training crawler.
  • Microsoft Copilot draws on Bing's index, which Bingbot builds.
  • Google AI Overviews and AI Mode use Google's regular search index, built by Googlebot.

None of them publish a recipe for which passage wins. The pattern across all of them is consistent, though: a direct answer near the top of the page, specific facts, a clear subject in every sentence, and a source that looks trustworthy because other sites say the same things about it.

How is GEO different from SEO?

GEO is SEO with a sharper focus on being quotable and consistent. Classic SEO aims for a ranked link that people click. GEO aims for your page to be one of the sources an AI answer is built from, and ideally the one it names. You'll also hear answer engine optimization (AEO) used for roughly the same idea, and my glossary keeps a one-line definition of GEO for when you need to explain it to someone else.

SEO and GEO side by side
AspectSEOGEO
The goalA ranked link people clickA cited passage inside an AI answer

The overlap is the point. Almost everything that helps you get cited also helps you rank, so GEO work is rarely wasted, even if the way people search changes again next year.

What does Google say about optimizing for AI search?

Google's position is that there's no separate trick: the SEO practices that make a page eligible for Search are the same ones that make it eligible for AI Overviews and AI Mode. Its guidance for site owners, AI features and your website on Google Search Central, makes several points worth knowing before anyone sells you “AI optimization”:

  • Indexed and snippet-eligible. A page has to be indexed and allowed to show a snippet in Search. There are no extra technical requirements.
  • Unique, people-first content matters most, especially content that shows real experience.
  • No special AI files or markup. There's no AI-only schema to add. Structured data still helps machines read your facts, and it has to match what's visible.
  • The basics still count: crawl access, internal links, good page experience.
  • Scaled pages are risky. Mass-produced pages made to manipulate rankings can break its spam policies, whoever or whatever wrote them.

Google has also said its AI features can link to a wider range of sources than a classic results page. That's the opening for smaller businesses: a precise, honest answer on an otherwise modest site can still be picked for the question it answers best.

Can an AI quote your sentences on their own?

A sentence is quotable when it still makes sense, and is still true, after it's lifted out of your page. AI answers are stitched together from passages, so every sentence that carries a fact should name its subject, say something specific and not lean on the sentence before it. Try it on two drafts of the same paragraph from a fictional bookkeeping firm.

ExampleThe question an AI is answering

How much does bookkeeping cost for a small business?

Each sentence below is marked with whether an AI could quote it on its own, and why.

ADraft A: the usual way

  1. At Northfield, we believe every business deserves total financial clarity.Not quotableA belief, not a fact. There's nothing here an answer could repeat.
  2. That's why our pricing is tailored, flexible and built around you.Not quotableStarts with “That's why,” so quoted alone it points at nothing, and the rest is adjectives.
  3. Northfield Books works with restaurants, shops and freelancers across Ohio.QuotableNames who does what, for whom and where. It's true wherever it's quoted.
  4. It really depends on a lot of things, so it's best to reach out.Not quotableDodges the question. “It” and “a lot of things” are never named.

BDraft B: answer-first

  1. Small-business bookkeeping is usually priced by the month, based on how many transactions and accounts you have.QuotableNames the subject and answers the question in one sentence.
  2. The biggest cost drivers are transaction volume, payroll, sales tax filings and how tidy the books are when you start.QuotableA specific list an answer can repeat word for word.
  3. This makes quotes hard to compare.Not quotableStarts with “This.” Quoted alone, nobody knows what “this” is.
  4. Northfield Books quotes a fixed monthly fee after a short review of your accounts.QuotableSays who does what and how, even with the rest of the page gone.

The AI answer these drafts could feed

  • Small-business bookkeeping is usually priced by the month, based on how many transactions and accounts you have.1
  • The biggest cost drivers are transaction volume, payroll, sales tax filings and how tidy the books are when you start.1
  • Northfield Books quotes a fixed monthly fee after a short review of your accounts.1
  • Northfield Books works with restaurants, shops and freelancers across Ohio.1

1Source: northfieldbooks.example, a fictional firm

Notice the pattern. The quotable sentences answer something a customer would actually ask, and they'd survive word for word in a newsletter, a review or an AI answer. That habit is the practical core of GEO writing, and it makes your pages easier for people to skim, too.

How do I get my business cited by AI search? Seven steps

Make your site easy to reach, easy to read and easy to trust, then give other sites reasons to talk about you. Here are the seven steps in the order that usually unblocks the most.

  1. 1

    Let the right crawlers in

    Check your robots.txt file and any firewall or bot-protection settings. Search crawlers like OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Claude-SearchBot, Bingbot and Googlebot need access if you want those tools to cite you. Training crawlers like GPTBot and ClaudeBot are a separate decision, and you can go either way.

  2. 2

    Make every key page indexable and fast

    One canonical URL per page, a current sitemap, no important text hidden behind scripts, logins or images, and pages that load quickly on a phone. Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools show what's actually indexed. If you're about to go live, my website launch checklist covers all of this.

  3. 3

    Lead with the answer

    Put a 40–70-word answer that stands on its own right under each page's main heading, and phrase subheadings as the questions customers ask. Then go into detail.

  4. 4

    State your facts the same way everywhere

    Business name, what you do, where you work, who runs it and how to reach you should match on your site, your Google Business Profile, directories and social profiles. Conflicting facts make a source look less reliable.

  5. 5

    Add structured data that matches the page

    Structured data such as Organization, LocalBusiness, Service, Product, Article and FAQ markup states your facts in a format machines can't misread. Keep it identical to what visitors see. It's part of every AI search optimization project I take on.

  6. 6

    Publish what only you can write

    Your process, your real examples, your opinions, the questions customers ask you on calls. First-hand detail is hard to copy, and it's exactly the kind of content Google says it wants to show.

  7. 7

    Earn mentions on sites people trust

    Reviews on the platforms your customers use, local press, industry associations, partners. AI tools weigh what others say about you, not only what you say about yourself.

What GEO tactics should I skip?

Anything that only works on machines. If you'd be embarrassed for a customer to see it, it's a bet against how search engines and AI tools say they judge content.

  • “AI-optimized” pages stuffed with keywords

    Repeating a phrase doesn't make a passage more quotable. It makes it worse.

  • Hundreds of near-identical location pages

    Thin pages made at scale are exactly what spam policies are written for.

  • Hidden text, or content shown only to bots

    Showing crawlers something people can't see has broken search guidelines for decades.

  • Fake reviews, or FAQ markup that isn't on the page

    Structured data has to match visible content, and in the US the FTC's rule against fake reviews applies too.

  • Paying for “guaranteed” ChatGPT placement

    Nobody controls organic AI answers. A guarantee is a sales line, not a service.

Tip

llms.txt costs an hour, not a strategy. An llms.txt file is a proposed plain-text map of your site for AI tools. Google has said its Search doesn't use it, and there's little public evidence that AI crawlers rely on it yet. It's quick and harmless to add, so I include one on the sites I build, but I never treat it as a reason anyone will cite you.

How can I tell whether AI search mentions my business?

There's no single dashboard for it yet, so combine three checks: ask the assistants, read your analytics and watch Search Console.

  1. Ask the questions your customers ask. Once a month, put the same handful of real questions to ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot and Google's AI Mode, and log whether you're mentioned, cited or linked, and what's said about you. Answers vary by person, place and day, so look for trends rather than single results.
  2. Check referrals in your analytics. Visits from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, copilot.microsoft.com and gemini.google.com show up as referral traffic in most analytics tools. The numbers are small for most sites today, but the trend tells you something.
  3. Watch Google Search Console. Clicks and impressions from Google's AI features are counted in its Performance report. How much of that it breaks out separately has been changing, so check what your account shows.

And remember what you control. If a check shows an assistant describing your business wrongly, the fix is almost always a clearer page, more consistent facts, or a mention on a site it already trusts.

Ask me directly →
Is GEO just a new name for SEO?

Partly. Google itself says optimizing for its AI features is still SEO, and every AI search tool depends on pages it can crawl and trust. The new part is emphasis: writing passages that can be quoted on their own, keeping business facts consistent across the web, and managing access for AI crawlers deliberately.

Does my website need to rank on page one to be cited by AI?

Not necessarily, but it helps. AI tools retrieve sources from search indexes, and Google's AI features draw on its core ranking systems, so pages that rank well are more likely to be considered. A page with a uniquely clear answer to a specific question can still be cited for that question.

Do AI assistants read my whole website?

Usually not in one go. Most fetch the specific pages relevant to a question, either from their index or live, and use the passages that best answer it. That's why each important page should stand on its own, with a clear answer near the top.

Should small businesses care about GEO yet?

Yes, in proportion. The foundations, a fast, crawlable site with clear answers and consistent facts, help classic search as well, so the work isn't wasted if AI habits change. Start with the pages customers actually ask about: services, pricing approach, location and contact.

Can I pay to appear in ChatGPT or AI Overviews answers?

Not in the organic answers. Some AI tools and search engines show ads near or within AI experiences in some markets, but citations in the answer itself aren't sold. Be skeptical of anyone offering guaranteed placement.
