The web design glossary, A to Z in plain English.

This web design glossary explains the 62 terms that come up most when you plan or run a website.

They cover design, development, search, AI search, e-commerce and accessibility. Each definition is one or two plain-English sentences, so you can follow a conversation with a designer or developer without nodding along. Terms run alphabetically: jump with the letter keys or search.

Which web terms do people mix up?

Six pairs that sound alike and get swapped in meetings all the time. Flip a card to its partner, read the difference in one line, then jump to the full entries.Each card shows both terms, the difference in one line, and links to the full entries.

  • 01Search

    SEO

    Earning a place in the list of search results.

    Full entry: SEO ↓ GEO

    GEO

    Earning a mention inside the answers AI tools write.

    Full entry: GEO ↓ SEO

    The difference SEO earns a place in search results; GEO earns a mention inside AI answers. Both start from the same well-built page.

  • 02Design

    UI

    The controls you see and touch: layout, type, buttons, forms.

    Full entry: UI design ↓ UX

    UX

    How easy and clear the whole journey feels, start to finish.

    Full entry: UX design ↓ UI

    The difference UI is the controls you touch; UX is how the whole trip feels. UI is one part of UX.

  • 03Setup

    Domain

    The address people type to find you.

    Full entry: Domain name ↓ Hosting

    Hosting

    The place your website’s files live and get served from.

    Full entry: Hosting ↓ Domain

    The difference The domain is your street address; hosting is the building. You can move to a new building and keep the address.

  • 04Editing

    CMS

    An admin screen for editing your text, images and pages.

    Full entry: CMS ↓ Builder

    Page builder

    A drag-and-drop tool that assembles pages from ready-made blocks.

    Full entry: Page builder ↓ CMS

    The difference A CMS manages your content; a page builder assembles the layout. A custom site can have a CMS without any page builder.

  • 05People

    Accessibility

    Can everyone use it, including people with disabilities?

    Full entry: Accessibility ↓ Usability

    Usability

    How easily the people it’s for can get things done.

    Closest entry: UX design ↓ Access

    The difference Accessibility asks whether everyone can use it; usability asks how easily they can. A good site needs both.

  • 06Moving pages

    301

    This page has moved for good. Go to the new address.

    Full entry: 301 redirect ↓ 302

    302

    This page is away for now and will be back.

    Full entry: 302 redirect ↓ 301

    The difference A 301 tells search engines to show the new address from now on; a 302 tells them to keep the old one, because it’s coming back.

What does your designer actually mean?

Three sentences of the kind you hear on any web project. Tap any underlined term and it turns into plain English right where it stands, or translate everything at once. Every term has its full entry in the glossary below.Each one is printed twice, as you’ll hear it and in plain English, and every term links to its entry below.

  1. 01Heard on a kickoff call

    “We’ll wireframe the key pages, agree on the information architecture, then turn the approved prototype into a site that’s responsive and mobile-first.”

    In plain English“We’ll sketch simple layouts of the key pages, agree on the way the content is organized, then turn the approved clickable model into a site that’s sized to fit every screen and designed for phones first.”

  2. 02From a project proposal

    “Content lives in a CMS, every page carries JSON-LD, and the main CTA keeps working thanks to progressive enhancement.”

    In plain English“Content lives in a simple admin screen you edit yourself, every page carries a summary search engines can read, and the main button keeps working thanks to a build that still works if a script fails.”

  3. 03In a launch-day email

    “We’re live: CLS is in the green, the XML sitemap is in Search Console, and robots.txt lets the AI crawlers in.”

    In plain English“We’re live: how much the page jumps while it loads is in the green, the list of all your pages is in Google’s free tool for site owners, and the site’s rule file for bots lets the bots that AI search tools send in.”

Numbers0–9

2 terms

301 redirect#

An instruction on the server that permanently sends visitors and search engines from an old URL to a new one. It’s the standard way to keep links and search visibility when pages move.

Related

  • 302 redirect
  • Canonical URL
  • Website redesign services
302 redirect#

A temporary redirect that sends visitors to another URL while signaling that the original address will come back. Use it for short-term moves, not permanent ones.

Related

  • 301 redirect
  • Indexing

Letter AA

8 terms

A/B testing#

Showing two versions of a page or element to different visitors at the same time and comparing which one achieves the goal better. It needs enough traffic to produce a reliable result.

Related

  • Conversion rate
  • Call to action
  • Landing page design
Accessibility (a11y)#

Designing and building websites so people with disabilities can perceive, understand, navigate and use them, including people using screen readers, keyboards or magnification. “a11y” is shorthand: 11 letters between the “a” and the “y.”

Related

  • WCAG
  • ARIA
  • ADA
ADA#

The Americans with Disabilities Act, a 1990 US civil-rights law that prohibits discrimination on the basis of disability. US courts and the Department of Justice have applied it to websites, although there is still no specific web rule for private businesses.

Related

  • Accessibility
  • WCAG
  • Accessibility and the ADA (guide)
AEO (answer engine optimization)#

Making content easy for systems that answer questions directly, such as AI assistants, voice assistants and featured snippets, to find and use. It overlaps heavily with GEO and builds on SEO.

Related

  • GEO
  • Featured snippet
  • SEO
AI crawler#

An automated bot that fetches web pages for an AI company, either to train models or to find sources for live answers. Many AI companies run separate crawlers for each purpose, which robots.txt can allow or block individually.

Related

  • Crawler
  • robots.txt
  • AI search optimization
AI Overviews#

AI-generated summaries that Google shows at the top of some search results, with links to the sources used. Pages need to be indexed and eligible for a snippet to be considered as a source.

Related

  • GEO
  • Featured snippet
  • What is GEO? (guide)
Alt text#

A short written description of an image, stored in the HTML, that screen readers read aloud and that shows if the image fails to load. Decorative images get empty alt text so they’re skipped.

Related

  • Accessibility
  • ARIA
ARIA#

Accessible Rich Internet Applications, a set of HTML attributes that describe the role and state of custom interface elements to assistive technology. The first rule of ARIA is to use a native HTML element instead whenever one exists.

Related

  • Accessibility
  • WCAG
  • Progressive enhancement

Letter BB

1 term

Breadcrumbs#

A short trail of links showing where a page sits in a site’s structure, such as Home › Guides › Glossary. They help visitors move up a level and help search engines understand the hierarchy.

Related

  • Information architecture
  • Structured data

Letter CC

9 terms

Call to action (CTA)#

A button or link that invites the visitor to take a specific next step, such as “Start a project” or “Add to cart.” A strong page has one primary CTA.

Related

  • Conversion rate
  • Landing page
Canonical URL#

The preferred address of a page when the same content is reachable at several URLs, declared with a rel="canonical" tag. Search engines treat it as a strong hint about which version to show.

Related

  • 301 redirect
  • Indexing
Checkout#

The steps in an online store between the cart and a confirmed order: shipping details, delivery choice and payment. Every unnecessary field or surprise cost in checkout is a reason to leave.

Related

  • Payment gateway
  • SKU
  • E-commerce website design
Chrome UX Report (CrUX)#

Google’s public dataset of how real Chrome users experience websites, including their Core Web Vitals. PageSpeed Insights and Search Console use it for their field data, summarized over the last 28 days.

Related

  • Field data
  • PageSpeed Insights
  • Core Web Vitals
CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift)#

A Core Web Vitals metric that measures how much visible content jumps around unexpectedly while a page loads. Google considers 0.1 or less a good score.

Related

  • Core Web Vitals
  • LCP
  • INP
CMS (content management system)#

Software that lets you edit a website’s text, images and pages through an admin screen instead of code. WordPress is the best-known example; headless CMSs deliver content to a separately built front end.

Related

  • Headless
  • Page builder
  • Custom website vs website builder
Conversion rate#

The percentage of visitors who complete a goal, such as a purchase, sign-up or inquiry. It’s only meaningful when the goal is clearly defined and tracked.

Related

  • A/B testing
  • Call to action
Core Web Vitals#

Three Google metrics for real-world page experience: loading (LCP), responsiveness (INP) and visual stability (CLS). They’re measured from real Chrome users and reported in Search Console.

Related

  • LCP
  • INP
  • CLS
  • Core Web Vitals (guide)
Crawler#

A program that automatically visits web pages and follows links to discover and read content, like Googlebot or Bingbot. Crawling comes before indexing: a page that can’t be crawled can’t be shown.

Related

  • AI crawler
  • Indexing
  • robots.txt

Design system#

A shared library of reusable components, styles and rules (colors, type, spacing, buttons, forms) that keeps a website or product consistent as it grows.

Related

  • UI design
  • Prototype
DNS (Domain Name System)#

The internet’s address book, which translates a domain name like example.com into the server address where the site lives. DNS settings control where your website and email are delivered.

Related

  • Domain name
  • Hosting
Domain name#

The address people type to reach your website, such as zstore-ai.com. You register it yearly with a registrar; it’s separate from hosting.

Related

  • DNS
  • Hosting
  • HTTPS

Letter EE

2 terms

E-E-A-T#

Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness: the qualities Google’s search quality rater guidelines use to describe helpful, reliable content. It’s a framework for quality, not a single ranking score.

Related

  • SEO
  • Structured data
European Accessibility Act (EAA)#

An EU law (Directive 2019/882) that has required many products and services, including e-commerce, to meet accessibility requirements since June 28, 2025. It can apply to businesses outside the EU that sell to EU consumers; very small service businesses are exempt.

Related

  • ADA
  • WCAG
  • Accessibility

Letter FF

2 terms

Featured snippet#

A highlighted excerpt that some search results show above the regular listings, answering the query directly with text taken from one page.

Related

  • AEO
  • AI Overviews
  • Meta description
Field data#

Performance measurements collected from real visitors on their own devices and connections, rather than from a test. Core Web Vitals are judged on field data.

Related

  • Lab data
  • Chrome UX Report (CrUX)
  • Core Web Vitals (guide)

Letter GG

1 term

GEO (generative engine optimization)#

Improving how often and how accurately AI-driven search tools, such as ChatGPT search, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity, find, understand and cite your content. It builds on SEO fundamentals rather than replacing them.

Related

  • AEO
  • llms.txt
  • What is GEO? (guide)
  • AI search optimization

Letter HH

3 terms

Headless#

An architecture where the content or commerce back end is separated from the front end visitors see, connected through an API. It offers design freedom at the cost of more development and maintenance.

Related

  • CMS
  • Page builder
Hosting#

The service that stores your website’s files and serves them to visitors over the internet. It’s separate from your domain name, though some companies sell both.

Related

  • Domain name
  • DNS
  • HTTPS
HTTPS (SSL/TLS)#

The secure version of HTTP, which encrypts data between the browser and the website using a TLS certificate. Browsers flag sites without it as “Not secure.”

Related

  • Hosting
  • Domain name

Letter II

4 terms

IndexNow#

An open protocol that lets a website notify participating search engines, such as Bing and Yandex, the moment a page is added, changed or deleted. Google does not currently participate.

Related

  • XML sitemap
  • Indexing
  • Website launch checklist
Indexing#

When a search engine stores a crawled page in its database so it can appear in results. A page can be crawled but not indexed, for example if it’s marked noindex or judged a duplicate.

Related

  • Crawler
  • Canonical URL
  • Search Console
INP (Interaction to Next Paint)#

A Core Web Vitals metric that measures how quickly a page visibly responds after you tap, click or type. It replaced First Input Delay in March 2024; 200 milliseconds or less counts as good.

Related

  • Core Web Vitals
  • LCP
  • CLS
Information architecture#

How a website’s content is organized, grouped, labeled and connected, from the navigation menu to the page structure. Good information architecture lets visitors find things without thinking.

Related

  • Wireframe
  • Breadcrumbs

JSON-LD#

A format for adding structured data to a page as a script block that describes things like your organization, services, articles or products. It’s the format Google recommends for structured data.

Related

Letter LL

5 terms

Lab data#

Performance measurements from a test run in a controlled setting, such as Lighthouse loading a page on a simulated phone. It’s useful for finding causes, but it can differ from what real visitors experience.

Related

  • Field data
  • Lighthouse
Landing page#

A standalone page designed around one goal and one audience, usually reached from an ad, email or campaign. It removes distractions so the main action is obvious.

Related

  • Call to action
  • A/B testing
  • Landing page design
LCP (Largest Contentful Paint)#

A Core Web Vitals metric that measures how long the largest visible image or text block takes to appear. Google considers 2.5 seconds or less good.

Related

  • Core Web Vitals
  • CLS
  • INP
Lighthouse#

A free Google tool, built into Chrome and PageSpeed Insights, that tests a page and scores its performance, accessibility, SEO and best practices from 0 to 100. Its scores are lab results, not Core Web Vitals.

Related

  • Lab data
  • PageSpeed Insights
  • Core Web Vitals
llms.txt#

A proposed plain-text file at a site’s root that gives AI tools a short, curated map of the site’s most useful content. It’s a voluntary proposal: Google says its Search doesn’t use it, and support among AI tools is limited so far.

Related

  • GEO
  • robots.txt
  • AI crawler

Letter MM

2 terms

Meta description#

A short summary of a page in its HTML that search engines may show under the title in results. It doesn’t directly affect rankings but strongly affects whether people click.

Related

  • Title tag
  • Featured snippet
Mobile-first design#

Designing for the smallest screen first and then enhancing for larger ones. It forces clear priorities and matches how most people browse.

Related

  • Responsive design
  • Progressive enhancement

Page builder#

A drag-and-drop tool for assembling web pages from pre-made blocks without writing code, offered by platforms like Wix and Squarespace and as WordPress plugins. Fast to start, but often heavier and harder to make distinctive.

Related

PageSpeed Insights#

Google’s free tool at pagespeed.web.dev that shows a page’s real-visitor Core Web Vitals, when there’s enough data, alongside a Lighthouse lab test with suggestions.

Related

  • Lighthouse
  • Chrome UX Report (CrUX)
  • Field data
Payment gateway#

The service that securely passes a customer’s payment details from your checkout to the payment networks and returns approval or decline. Examples include Stripe, PayPal and Square.

Related

  • HTTPS
Progressive enhancement#

Building a page so its core content and functions work in the simplest browser setup, then layering richer features on top for browsers that support them. If the script fails, the page still works.

Related

  • ARIA
Prototype#

A clickable model of a website or app used to test structure and interactions before development. It can range from rough wireframes to a near-final design.

An approach where one website adapts its layout to any screen size, from phone to wide desktop, instead of having separate mobile and desktop sites.

A text file at a site’s root that tells crawlers which parts of the site they may or may not visit, with rules per bot. Well-behaved crawlers follow it, but it’s a request, not a lock.

Structured data that uses the Schema.org vocabulary to label what a page is about, such as an organization, product, article or FAQ. Search engines use it to understand pages and to power rich results.

Google’s free tool for site owners that shows which searches bring visitors, which pages are indexed, and any crawling, mobile or Core Web Vitals problems. Bing offers a similar tool, Bing Webmaster Tools.

Improving a website’s content, structure and technical setup so it’s easier for search engines to crawl, understand and rank, and more useful to the people searching.

Stock keeping unit: a unique code for each distinct product variant a store sells, such as a T-shirt in one size and color.

Information added to a page in a standardized, machine-readable format, usually JSON-LD with Schema.org types, so search engines and other tools can understand facts on the page without guessing.

The HTML element that names a page; it appears in the browser tab and usually as the clickable headline in search results. Each page should have a unique, descriptive one.

User interface design: the look and behavior of the parts people interact with, such as layout, type, color, buttons, forms and states.

4 short answers

Want the reasoning behind a term, not just the definition? The guides go deeper on AI search, launches and accessibility.

Ask me directly →
Is GEO the same as AEO?

They overlap almost completely. GEO (generative engine optimization) and AEO (answer engine optimization) both describe making content easy for AI-driven answers to find, understand and cite. Some people use AEO for featured snippets and voice answers too. In practice both build on solid SEO.

Why do web designers use so much jargon?

Partly habit and partly precision: terms like “LCP” or “canonical URL” name very specific things. A good designer translates them when they matter for your decisions. This glossary is here so you can ask better questions, not so you have to learn the trade.

Can I suggest a term for the glossary?

Yes, please do. If a term came up in a conversation with a designer, developer or marketer and left you puzzled, send it to me by email or WhatsApp and I’ll add a plain-English definition when I next update the page.

How are the definitions kept accurate?

Each definition is checked against primary sources where they exist, such as W3C documents for accessibility, Google Search Central for search, and official specifications for web standards. The page is updated when those sources change.
