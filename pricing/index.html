04Pricing · No hourly meters

Fixed-price website design, explained in plain words.

With fixed-price website design, every project gets one price based on the scope we agree, quoted after a short conversation. You get a written proposal with deliverables, timeline and price before anything starts, and changes are agreed together. Own the site outright, or choose a monthly plan where I host and look after it. Either way, launch includes 30 days of care, and no hourly meter ever runs.

How does fixed-price website design work?

By scope, not by the hour. Scope is the list we agree on: the pages, the features, the content and the tools your site connects to. The price is fixed for that list, so you know the total before you commit.

  1. 01

    The total, up front

    The full price is in the written proposal before any work starts. Nothing is added later without your yes.

  2. 02

    No meter running

    Questions, calls and small tweaks inside the scope never add to the bill, so ask as much as you like.

  3. 03

    Focus, for both of us

    Predictable for you, focused for me: the work goes into a site that does its job, not into tracking time.

What each factor adds to your proposal

Every factor below changes the work. Here's why, and the lines it adds to a written proposal.

Your goals

Every proposal starts here: what the site must achieve, and who it's for.

  • A summary of your goals and audience
  • The scope, including what's out of it
  • A timeline, the fixed price and what launch includes
Several unique page types

A page type is a distinct layout, like home, service, product or article. Each is designed once and reused, so the number of types matters more than the page count.

  • A sitemap with every page type named
  • A design for each page type, on phone and desktop
Content that isn't ready yet

Whether your copy and images exist, need editing or still need gathering. Content shapes the design, so the plan has to account for it.

  • A content plan: what each page needs, and from whom
  • Dates for when content is due, built into the timeline
Custom interaction and motion

Signature interactive pieces, animation and 3D take design and testing time on every screen size.

  • The interactive moments, described one by one
  • Reduced-motion versions, so everyone can use the site
An online store

Catalog structure, product variants, checkout and payment setup turn a website into a shop.

  • Product and collection page design
  • Checkout and payment setup through an established provider
  • A handoff so you can edit products yourself
Integrations with your tools

Forms, CRM, email, booking, payments and analytics each need connecting and testing.

  • A list of every tool the site connects to
  • What each connection does, and how it's tested
Editing the site yourself

When self-editing is part of the brief, the site gets a CMS and the content is structured for it.

  • A CMS for the pages you want to edit
  • Handoff guidance for editing on your own
More than one language or region

Each language adds translated content, layout checks and the setup search engines need to show the right version.

  • The languages and regions in scope
  • Language switching and hreflang setup
Moving from an existing site

A website redesign moves content and keeps old links working, so rankings and bookmarks survive the switch.

  • Content migration from your current site
  • A redirect map from old URLs to new ones
A fixed launch date

A hard deadline changes how the work is sequenced, and what launches first.

  • A milestone plan worked back from your date
  • What launches first, and what can follow in a second phase

What's in a proposal?

Everything you need to say yes with confidence, in writing, before any work starts. It follows the four-step process, so you can see what you get at each stage and when I'll need something from you.

ProposalFor your project

  1. Goals and audienceA short summary, so we agree on what the site is for.
  2. ScopePages, features and integrations, plus what's explicitly out of scope.
  3. Deliverables per stepWhat you get in Discover, Design, Develop and Launch.
  4. TimelineMilestones, and the dates I need content and feedback from you.
  5. The priceOne fixed price for the scope, or the setup and monthly fee if you choose the plan.
  6. How changes workAnything outside the scope is quoted before work on it starts.
  7. What launch includesGo-live, a personal handoff and 30 days of post-launch care.
  8. Care plan optionsWhat an optional monthly Care plan would cover for your site.

What happens if the scope changes?

We pause, price the change, and you decide. Small adjustments inside the agreed scope stay inside it. New features are quoted as an addition before any work on them starts.

  1. Pause

    I flag it first

    When a new idea comes up mid-project, I say so before building anything outside the agreed scope.

  2. Price

    You see it in writing

    What the change adds, how it moves the timeline, and its price, all before you commit.

  3. Decide

    You choose

    Add it now, save it for a later phase, or drop it. No surprise invoices.

What's included in the 30 days of post-launch care?

Included in every launch

A month to settle in, with me on hand. Real visitors always find something nobody planned for, so the first 30 days after launch are part of the project.

  • Fixes for anything that doesn't work as agreed.
  • Small adjustments once real visitors start using the site.
  • Help getting comfortable running and editing your site.
  • Answers to your questions as they come up.

After that, keep going with a Care plan, ask for help only when you need it, or run the site yourself.

Why don't you publish prices?

Because two “five-page websites” can be completely different projects.

A number without a scope would be too high for the first site or misleading for the second. A short conversation gets you a real number, in writing, for your project and nobody else's.

Fair questions.

7 straight answers

Money questions deserve plain answers. Anything else you'd like to know?

Ask me directly →
Do you charge by the hour?

No. Projects are priced as a fixed amount for an agreed scope, so you know the total before work begins and never watch a meter. If you later want something outside that scope, I quote it separately first, and you decide whether to add it.

Is the quote free?

Yes. The first conversation and the written proposal cost nothing and commit you to nothing. It's how we both find out whether the project is a good fit and what a sensible scope looks like.

Can we start smaller and add to the site later?

Yes, and it's often the smart move. A focused first version, like a landing page or a core set of pages, can launch sooner, and later phases get their own scope and fixed price. I design the first version so it can grow without rework.

Do I have to sign up for the Care plan?

No. The monthly Care plan is optional. Every launch already includes 30 days of post-launch care, and after that you can choose a plan, ask for help only when you need it, or manage the site yourself with the handoff guidance.

What can make a quote change after the proposal?

Only a change in scope that we agree on together, such as new pages, a new integration or a feature that wasn't in the brief. The proposal states what's included and what isn't, so any change is visible and quoted before work on it starts.

Who owns the website when it's finished?

With a fixed project price, you do: at handover the design, code and content are fully yours. On the monthly plan, I host and look after the site while the plan runs, and you can buy it out anytime to own it outright. Either way, the domain is registered in your name and the content you supplied is always yours.

What happens if I end the monthly plan?

You decide what comes next. Buy the site out and it becomes fully yours, design, code and content included. Or let the plan end, and the site is taken offline. Your domain and the content you supplied stay yours either way.
