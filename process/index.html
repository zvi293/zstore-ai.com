Where projects stall, and how we keep them moving

  • Before 06 · Build

    Content isn't ready

    Designs can be finished before the words and photos are, but a site can't launch on placeholder text. We plan content in week one and put a content date in the proposal.

  • Before 08 · QA

    Feedback arrives late

    Final checks start from your review notes, so long gaps push every later step. We agree review dates up front, and I send short, specific questions that are quick to answer.

  • Before 09 · Launch

    Access is missing

    Going live needs your domain, DNS settings and the accounts the site connects to. An access checklist in week one means nothing is missing on launch day.

001Step 01 / 04 · Discover

What happens in Discover?

Discover turns your idea into a plan we both agree on. It starts with a conversation about your business, who the site is for and what success looks like, and ends with a written proposal: the scope, a clear timeline and the price, as a fixed project price or a monthly plan, whichever suits you.

What happens

  • A discovery conversation about your business, your audience and the one thing the site must do well.
  • Creative direction: the feel you're after, the references you like and what to avoid.
  • A project roadmap: pages, features, integrations and the content each page needs.
  • A written proposal with scope, timeline and price. Here's how the fixed price works.

What you bring

  • Your goals, even rough ones
  • Websites you like, and a few you don't
  • Your logo, colors and any brand assets
  • Who gives feedback and signs off

You getA proposal in writing, before any work starts

0012Step 02 / 04 · Design

What happens in Design?

In Design, your website takes shape before any code is written. First a visual direction, then responsive layouts and an interactive prototype you can click through on your phone and your computer. We refine the details together until it feels right, within the agreed scope.

What happens

  • A visual direction: type, color, imagery and motion, tried on a real page of your content.
  • Responsive layouts for phones first, then tablets and desktops.
  • An interactive prototype you can click, so you judge the experience, not a flat picture.
  • Refinement together until it feels right, within the agreed scope.

How feedback works

  • Written comments, on your own time
  • A call in our shared hours when talking is faster
  • Review dates agreed up front, so nothing waits

You getA prototype you can click before anything is built

00123Step 03 / 04 · Develop

What happens in Develop?

In Develop, I build the approved design in hand-written code. That means the pages, the interactions and every integration the site needs. Then I test it on real phones and browsers, and you review the working site before anything goes live.

What happens

  • Custom development on open web standards: no templates, no page builders.
  • Interactions and motion that feel physical and respect reduced-motion settings.
  • Integrations: forms, payments, booking, analytics, or a CMS when you want to edit content yourself.
  • Quality checks on phones, tablets and desktops, plus speed, accessibility and SEO foundations.

Your part

  • Try the working site on your own devices
  • Send your notes by the agreed date
  • Share access to the tools the site connects to

Good to knowEvery one of my web design services runs through these same four steps.

001234Step 04 / 04 · Launch

What happens at Launch, and after?

Launch is a checklist, not a leap. I run the final checks, we go live when you're ready, and you get a personal handoff and 30 days of post-launch care. After that, an optional monthly Care plan keeps the site secure, fast and improving.

What happens

  • Final checks: forms, redirects, analytics, metadata and speed. My website launch checklist shows the full list.
  • Going live on your schedule, with the domain registered in your name.
  • A personal handoff: how the site works and how to make everyday changes.
  • 30 days of post-launch care: fixes for anything that doesn't work as agreed, small adjustments and help getting comfortable with the site.

After the 30 days

An optional monthly Care plan: hosting, security and uptime monitoring, backups, updates, small content changes and a short monthly report. Add or remove services anytime.

How long does a website project take?

A landing page usually takes 1–2 weeks and a full marketing site 4–6 weeks. The real timeline depends on scope, how ready your content is and how many tools the site connects to, and your proposal spells it out before we start.

What does the first conversation cover?

Your business, who the website is for, what it needs to achieve and what's already in place. I'll ask about examples you like and dislike, your timeline and anything that's non-negotiable. It's not a sales pitch: by the end you should know whether I'm the right fit and what a sensible scope looks like.

What's the most common reason projects slow down?

Content. Designs can be ready before the words and images are, and a site can't launch on placeholder text. Planning content early, and agreeing up front who writes what, is the best way to keep the timeline. The second most common reason is feedback that arrives in long gaps, which is why we agree review dates at the start.

How do you share progress during the project?

In clear written updates at each milestone, with links you can open on your phone and your computer, plus a call when a conversation will move things forward. Because the person writing the update is the person doing the work, there's no translation layer between what you see and what's happening.

Can we start before all the content is ready?

Yes. Discovery and much of the design can begin with a content plan and realistic placeholders for structure. Final design details and the build depend on real content, so we agree a date for it in the proposal and plan the timeline around it.

Do I need to be technical to work with you?

Not at all. I explain choices in plain English and handle the technical parts, from domains and hosting settings to integrations. You'll be asked for decisions about your business, not about code. If you want the technical detail, I'm happy to go as deep as you like.
