04Service · GEO & AEO

AI search optimization, built on foundations that last.

AI search optimization (also called GEO or AEO) means making your website easy for AI assistants and AI search features to find, understand and cite. I do it with foundations, not tricks: crawler access, structured data, answer-first content and consistent facts.

Start a project→ Try the crawler loupe↓

01The short answer

What is AI search optimization?

It's work that helps the AI systems that answer questions, like ChatGPT search, Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot and Claude, find, understand and cite your pages.

Those tools don't hand out ten blue links. They read a handful of pages, write one answer and credit a few sources. The job is to make your pages the easy, trustworthy ones to read and quote: reachable by their crawlers, clear about who you are and what you do, and written so a single passage makes sense on its own.

If you want the concept in depth, I wrote a plain-English guide to what generative engine optimization is and how AI search chooses its sources.

How does AI search optimization build on SEO?

It starts with SEO done thoroughly, then adds extra attention to being quotable. The foundation is the same; a few layers on top are new.

Google's guidance for site owners says there are no extra requirements or special optimizations for its AI Overviews and AI Mode: a page has to be indexed and eligible to appear in Search with a snippet, and the usual SEO best practices apply. So the classic foundation isn't optional. It's most of the job.

Other AI search tools run their own crawlers and indexes, and they quote passages rather than rank pages. That adds three layers worth doing on purpose:

  1. SEOThe classic foundationCrawlable, indexable, fast, accessible and genuinely helpful pages.
  2. +1AI search crawlers let inOAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot allowed deliberately, not by accident.
  3. +2The same facts everywhereName, services, location and contact details that match on your site and your profiles.
  4. +3Answer-first passagesA direct answer under each heading that still makes sense when quoted alone.

The full side-by-side lives in my guide: how GEO is different from SEO.

What do you actually do in an AI search optimization project?

Seven pieces of work, on a site I build or on the one you already have. You get a fixed project price based on the scope, or a monthly plan, whatever suits you.

01/ 07

  1. 01

    Crawl and index audit

    I check your robots.txt, XML sitemaps, canonical tags, redirects and stray noindex tags, so every page you care about can be found. AI search crawlers end up allowed or blocked on purpose, never by accident.

  2. 02

    Structured data that matches the page

    Organization, Service or Product, Article, FAQ and Breadcrumb structured data in JSON-LD, written from what's visible on each page. Nothing invented, nothing hidden.

  3. 03

    Answer-first rewrites

    Key pages open with a 40 to 70-word answer, use the questions people actually ask as headings, and keep each passage clear enough to be quoted alone.

  4. 04

    One set of business facts

    Your name, location, contact details, founder and description, aligned across the site and the profiles that describe you, such as your Google Business Profile.

  5. 05

    llms.txt and llms-full.txt

    A plain-text map of your site for AI tools, presented honestly: an llms.txt file is an inexpensive bet some tools may read, not a ranking factor, and Google says its Search doesn't use it.

    Cheap bet
  6. 06

    Performance and accessibility fixes

    Fast, semantic, accessible pages are easier for every crawler to fetch and parse, and better for every visitor. It's the same craft behind a custom website design project.

  7. 07

    IndexNow

    Whenever a page changes, Bing and the other participating search engines get an instant notice, so updates are discovered sooner instead of waiting for the next crawl.

Should I block AI crawlers or let them in?

Your call, split two ways

It's a business choice, and you can split it: allow the search crawlers that can cite you, and decide separately about the crawlers that collect training data.

Who's who

As each company documents its crawlers at the time of writing, OpenAI's OAI-SearchBot finds pages for ChatGPT search while GPTBot gathers training data. Anthropic's Claude-SearchBot serves search in Claude, and ClaudeBot collects training data. PerplexityBot powers Perplexity's search results.

Google-Extended isn't a separate crawler. It's a robots.txt token that controls whether Google may use your content to train Gemini models and ground Gemini answers, and it doesn't affect Google Search, AI Overviews included.

One caveat: robots.txt is a request, not a lock. Reputable crawlers follow it; it can't enforce anything on its own.

With a few honest instruments, because no single dashboard shows AI visibility yet. I won't promise a ranking, a spot in ChatGPT or a number of mentions. I'll show you where to look and what changed.

Google Search Console counts clicks and impressions from AI features such as AI Overviews and AI Mode in its Performance report, though it doesn't always break them out separately. Watch the pages you improved.

Fair questions.

6 straight answers

Wondering how this applies to your site?

Ask me directly →
Can you guarantee my business will appear in ChatGPT or Google's AI answers?

No, and be wary of anyone who does. AI systems choose their sources on their own terms, and those terms change often. What I can do is remove the obstacles and strengthen the signals they rely on: access, clear structure, accurate facts and genuinely useful answers. That improves your odds everywhere at once, classic search included.

Does structured data (schema markup) help with AI search?

It helps machines read the facts on a page without guessing, and it's still the route to rich results in Google. Google says structured data isn't required for its AI features and that there's no special AI markup, so I treat schema as part of a clean foundation, always matching what's visible on the page, never as a shortcut.

Do I need an llms.txt file?

It's optional. llms.txt is a proposed plain-text map of your site for AI tools. Google says its Search doesn't use it, and so far there's little public evidence that AI crawlers request it often. It takes little effort to add, so I include one as an inexpensive bet, not as a strategy.

How long until changes show up in AI answers?

There's no fixed timeline. Pages first need to be recrawled and re-indexed, which can take days to weeks depending on the engine and your site. IndexNow and Google Search Console help with discovery, but each AI system refreshes its sources on its own schedule.

Can you do AI search optimization on a website you didn't build?

Yes. Most of the work, like crawler access, structured data, content structure and performance, can be done on an existing site. If the platform limits what's possible, I'll say so in the audit and suggest the smallest change that removes the limit. Sometimes that's a redesign without losing search visibility.

Does AI search matter for local service businesses?

Yes. People increasingly ask assistants for recommendations like “a good accountant near me.” Accurate business details on your site and profiles (such as your Google Business Profile), clear service pages and genuine reviews on the platforms where customers leave them all feed those answers.
