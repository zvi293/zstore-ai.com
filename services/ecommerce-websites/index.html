01Three moments

What makes an online store easy to buy from?

Clarity at the three moments that matter: finding the product, trusting it, and paying for it. Get those right and a store feels easy, whatever it sells: product pages that answer questions before they're asked, a short path from cart to payment through an established, secure payment provider, and pages that load fast on the phone, where so much shopping starts.

  1. 1 FIND-01

    Find it

    Collections named the way your customers think, product cards that show what matters at a glance, and menus that work under one thumb.

    Collections · menus · search
  2. 2 TRUST-02

    Trust it

    Product pages that answer size, fit, material, care, shipping and returns before anyone has to ask. Real photos, honest details, no surprises.

    Product pages
  3. 3 PAY-03

    Pay for it

    Full costs shown before checkout, guest checkout wherever possible, and payment through an established, secure provider. Industry surveys consistently list surprise costs at checkout among the reasons people abandon a cart.

    Cart · checkout

05Mobile first

How do product pages work on a phone?

In one column, in the order a shopper decides: photos, name, price, options, the button, then the details. The rest of the work is keeping that column fast, calm and easy to tap.

  • A gallery that swipes, with sized WebP images so the main photo arrives fast as the page's Largest Contentful Paint.
  • Price and options above the first scroll, each variant named the way people shop and mapped to its own SKU.
  • A sticky add-to-cart bar that slides up once the main button scrolls away, as in the thumb test.
  • Tap targets of at least 44 pixels, readable type and real contrast: accessibility for online stores starts here.
  • Details that answer questions: size, materials, care, shipping and returns, in short sections that open on tap.

06Search and AI

Can search and AI answers find my products?

They can when every product page is crawlable, clearly written and described with product structured data. No one can promise a spot in an AI answer, so I build the foundations every engine reads:

  • Product structured data in JSON-LD: name, images, description, SKU and brand, plus the offer details (price and availability) that product results need, all generated from your catalog so it never drifts.
  • Clean, permanent product URLs, with redirects whenever anything moves.
  • Descriptive copy that answers real questions: the sentences AI assistants quote.
  • Fast pages, an XML sitemap and crawler access set on purpose.

Store questions.

5 straight answers

Selling something I haven't covered here?

Ask me directly →
Can you move my existing store to a new design without losing products or orders?

Yes, and that move is planned before any design work starts. Product data is exported and brought across, your order and customer history is exported from your current system and kept, old product URLs get redirects to their new addresses, and I test checkout end to end before launch. The exact steps depend on where your store runs today, so they're spelled out in the proposal.

Which payment methods can my store accept?

It depends on the payment provider and the countries you sell in. Established providers support the major cards, and many add wallets like Apple Pay and Google Pay plus local payment methods. I connect the provider you choose, or help you choose one, and design the checkout flow around it so paying feels like part of your store.

Do I need a separate landing page for a product launch?

Often it helps. A launch landing page focuses on one product and one action, while the store handles browsing and buying. Because I build both, the launch page can share your store's design and send people straight to the right product or a pre-order flow.

Will I be able to add products and run sales myself?

Yes. When managing the catalog yourself is part of the brief, and for most stores it is, I set up a simple editor for products and content. Adding products, changing prices and running a sale shouldn't need me. At handoff I walk you through it, and you get written guidance for the tasks you'll repeat.

Do you help with product photography or copy?

I help you plan them, and we decide together who creates them. Tell me in your brief what you already have. If photography or copywriting is missing, we'll settle how it gets done before design starts, because content readiness shapes the timeline.
