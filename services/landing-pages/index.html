01The short answer

What is a landing page, and when do I need one?

A landing page is a focused page for one audience and one action, usually reached from an ad, an email, a launch post or a QR code. Unlike a homepage, it doesn’t introduce everything you do. It makes one case, clearly, and asks for one thing.

It’s the right tool for

  • A product launch
  • Paid ads
  • Event or webinar sign-ups
  • A waitlist
  • Pre-orders
  • Testing an idea before a full site

When it isn’t

If visitors need to compare several services or get to know your company over time, you need a full custom website. If they came to browse and buy products, that’s your online store. A landing page can still lead into either one.

02The order of questions

Put the page in order.

A landing page is a conversation. Visitors arrive with questions in a fairly predictable order, and each section should answer the next one. Move the sections until every question gets its answer.

Who’s arriving?

People who’ve never heard of you, arriving from an ad, a search or a QR code. They need the whole story.Your email list, followers or past customers. They already know you, so the offer can come first.

There’s no single right order. Cold visitors need the whole story before they act. Warm ones, like your email list, can see the offer and the button within a scroll: Headline & promise, Offer details, The action, Hero image, How it works, Objections answered, then Proof. I decide the order with you before a single pixel is drawn, and learn more about writing it down in the website brief template.

03Conversion

What makes a landing page convert?

Message match, one clear action, and proof that answers doubt. Everything else on the page supports those three, or it goes.

  1. 01Message match

    The headline repeats the promise of the ad, email or post that brought them, so they know at a glance they’re in the right place.

  2. 02Plain-words value

    What it is, who it’s for and why it’s better, said simply before anything clever.

  3. 03One action, repeated

    One primary call to action, repeated down the page. Never three buttons competing for the same click.

  4. 04Fewer exits

    Little or no navigation, so the only way forward is the step the page exists for.

  5. 05Honest proof

    Real customer words, real numbers, real names, used with permission. Never invented testimonials.

  6. 06Fast on a phone

    Many visitors arrive on a phone, often on a patchy connection. A page that stalls loses them before the headline appears.

  7. 07A short form

    Only the fields you’ll actually use. Every extra field is one more reason to stop.

Every audience is different, so after launch we look at real behavior and decide together what’s worth testing, with A/B testing when there’s enough traffic to trust the result.

06Speed

How do you keep a landing page fast?

By writing only the code the page needs, and loading the most important thing first. Speed isn’t a plugin you add at the end. It comes from what you leave out.

  • Hand-written HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no page-builder scripts riding along.
  • Images sized for each screen in modern formats like WebP, never one huge file for everyone.
  • The largest element loads first, which is what LCP measures.
  • Space reserved for everything, so nothing jumps while the page loads (CLS).
  • Third-party tags only when they earn their place, loaded after the page is usable.

Just ask me →
Should my landing page have a navigation menu?

Usually a minimal one, or none at all. Every extra link is a way to leave without taking the action the page exists for. I keep the logo, perhaps a link back to your main site in the footer, and put everything a visitor needs on the page itself.

Can you build several versions of a landing page for testing?

Yes. The page is built from reusable sections, so a variant with a different headline, offer or order of sections is quick to make. Tell me which testing tool you use, or we agree before launch how traffic is split and how results are measured.

Where will my landing page live: on my main domain or a new one?

Either works. A page on your existing domain (for example, yoursite.com/launch) keeps everything under one brand, while a separate domain can suit a standalone campaign. We choose based on where your traffic comes from and how long the page needs to live.

What do I need to have ready before we start?

The goal (what counts as success), who the page is for, the offer, and any proof you can genuinely share, like product photos, demo footage or real customer quotes you have permission to use. If the copy isn't written yet, we work out the flow of questions together first.

Can the form send leads straight to my tools?

Usually, yes. A form can deliver to your inbox and to the tools you already use, such as a CRM, an email platform or a spreadsheet, depending on what those tools support. We map where every lead should go before the build.
