Do I need a refresh or a full redesign?

Refresh when the structure works and the look or speed doesn't. Rebuild when the structure, platform or message no longer fits. “Redesign” is a spectrum of five depths. Each step changes more, keeps less, and adds more migration care. Sometimes that's a focused refresh, sometimes a full rebuild: we decide together, based on your goals.

How do you redesign a website without losing search rankings?

By treating URLs and content as assets. I map every old URL, keep or improve what ranks, send the rest to their new homes with permanent 301 redirects, and check everything after launch.

  1. 01

    Baseline. Export what Google Search Console and analytics show today: top pages, search queries and the links pointing at you.

  2. 02

    Crawl. Crawl the old site so no page, image or PDF gets forgotten.

  3. 03

    Map. Give every old URL a destination: the same address, a new one, or the closest match.

  4. 04

    Protect. Keep the titles, headings and search intent of the pages that bring in traffic.

  5. 05

    Redirect. Write permanent 301 redirects and test every one before launch.

  6. 06

    Submit. Publish the new sitemap and submit it in Search Console.

  7. 07

    Watch. Track 404 errors and search performance in the weeks after launch, and fix whatever shows up.

Good to know

Some movement in rankings after a relaunch is normal while search engines re-crawl the site. A well-planned migration settles. For the full list of launch-day checks, redirects included, see the website launch checklist.

Can you redesign my site on the platform I already use?

If your site is already hand-coded, or runs on an open-source CMS that still fits, yes: a polish or a refresh can happen right where it is. If it lives on a website builder or a heavy theme, I rebuild it by hand on open web standards, and your domain, your content and your URLs come with you.

If someone on your team edits the site often, I connect a content management system so they can keep doing it, and I show them how before handover.

  • Your domain stays registered in your name.
  • Your content is always yours, whatever we change around it.

What should I prepare before a redesign?

Access to your accounts, an honest list of what you like and dislike about the current site, the questions customers ask most, and the content you want to keep. That's enough for a precise audit and an accurate quote.

Will my old links stop working after the redesign?

Not if the move is planned. Every important old URL either stays the same or gets a permanent (301) redirect to its new home, which sends visitors and search engines to the right page. I test the redirects before launch and watch for broken links in the weeks after.

Can we redesign in stages instead of all at once?

Yes, when the site's structure allows it. A staged redesign might start with the homepage and the pages that bring in the most leads, then move on to the rest. It spreads the work and lets you see results early, though it needs a shared design system so old and new pages don't clash.

What will you need access to?

Usually your current website admin, hosting and domain settings (DNS), Google Search Console and analytics. You stay the owner of every account. Wherever the platform allows, I ask for access as a separate user, so you can remove it when we're done.

Will my current content be reused?

What works is reused, and what doesn't is improved. In discovery I go through your existing pages to find what earns traffic, answers real customer questions or converts, and we decide page by page what to keep, rewrite, merge or retire.

How do I know if my site is worth redesigning at all?

Look for signs like a site that's slow or awkward on phones, an outdated look that undersells you, content you can't update, or pages that don't lead anywhere. If only one or two of those apply, a focused fix may be enough, and I'll tell you so.
