01Concept study · not client work

An architecture firm website paced like a walk.

Atelier Studio is a concept study in architecture firm website design, not client work. I asked how an architecture firm's site could feel as considered as the spaces it designs. My answer is image-led: a site you move through at the pace of a slow walk through a building.

02The brief I gave myself

What question did this study start with?

How should an architecture studio feel on a screen?

Architects spend months on how a building receives you: where the light falls, how long the hallway is, what you see first. Then the website often drops all of that into a grid of thumbnails. I wanted the site to feel as considered online as the spaces the studio creates.

So the study tells the story in a quieter, image-led way. Photographs lead and words follow. Each project gets room to breathe, and the interface stays out of the way, the way good architecture does.

03Walk the plan

Room 01 · Entrance

The hero: one photograph, one line.

You arrive the way you'd arrive at a building: one wide photograph of the work, the studio's name and a single line of type. No slider, no list of services. The menu is three small words in the corner, so the first thing you meet is architecture.

Room 02 · Gallery hall

The project index: walk past the work.

Projects hang like pieces in a gallery: one per row, a large image, a name and a place in small type. The rows arrive one at a time as you scroll, so the index reads as a walk past the work, not a wall of thumbnails.

Room 03 · Studio

The studio: people and principles.

Behind the projects sits one quiet page about who designs and how they think: a short statement, three working principles and the team by role. It answers the question every client has before they call: who will I be working with?

Room 04 · Courtyard

The courtyard: a pause with nothing to read.

Halfway through, the site stops talking. One full-width photograph and a single line, like stepping into a courtyard between rooms. It resets the eye before the last part of the visit, and it asks nothing of the visitor.

Room 05 · Contact room

The contact room: one clear way in.

The visit ends where a real one would, at the front desk: an inquiry form with four fields, an email address and a phone number. Nothing else competes, because by now the visitor has seen enough to decide.

04The sample board

What design decisions shape the site?

Four choices carry the whole study. Each one comes with its reason, because a style without a reason is only decoration.

05Beyond the study

What would an architecture firm actually need from its website?

The same things any serious studio site needs, done with care: project stories, a clear picture of the people, a simple way to get in touch, and images that look beautiful without slowing the page down.

How I design custom websites →
  1. A

    Project pages that read as stories

    The brief, the site, the approach and the result, when the client is happy to share it. A story shows a future client how you think; a gallery only shows what you made.

  2. B

    A clear studio and people page

    Who designs, how you work and what you believe, with real names and roles. People hire people, especially for a building they'll live with for decades.

  3. C

    Press and awards, only if they're real

    A short, current list builds trust. A padded or outdated one does the opposite, so leave the section out until there's something true to put in it.

  4. D

    A simple contact path

    One inquiry form, an email address, a phone number and the studio's address. Fewer choices make the first step easier to take.

  5. E

    Large images, served efficiently

    Modern formats like WebP, the right size for every screen, and lazy loading below the first screen. That's how an image-led site stays quick on a phone.

06Reflection

What did I learn from it?

Calm on a screen comes from pacing, never from a slow page.

It's tempting to make a calm site by slowing everything down: long fades, text that waits, images that take their time. That only feels tired. Waiting isn't the same as calm.

What works is the opposite. The page loads fast, then sets its own pace with space, scale and motion that answers you: a hero that holds still, rows that arrive one at a time, a pause before the contact room. Slowness on a screen has to be designed.

About the study.

4 straight answers

Something else on your mind?

Just ask me →
Is Atelier Studio a real architecture firm?

No. Atelier Studio is a fictional brand I invented for this concept study. The imagery is concept imagery and there's no client behind it. It exists to explore how an image-led, slower kind of website could work for architects and other studios whose work is visual.

Could this direction work for other creative studios?

Yes. The same principles, letting images lead, giving content room and keeping navigation quiet, suit interior designers, photographers, galleries and product designers. The details change with the work: type, palette and pacing would be redesigned around each brand.

How do you keep an image-heavy site fast?

By serving each image at the size the screen needs, in modern formats like WebP, and loading images below the first screen only when they're about to appear. The first, most important image loads with priority, so the page feels instant even though it's visual.

Can I commission a site in this style?

You can commission a site with this level of care. The concept itself stays a study, but its thinking, an editorial pace, image-led layouts and restrained navigation, can be adapted to your brand in a real project designed from scratch.
