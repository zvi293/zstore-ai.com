What do these projects have in common?

They're designed from scratch and hand-coded, mobile first. The brands look nothing alike; the way they're built is the same every time.

  1. 01

    No templates, no page builders

    Every layout starts on a blank page and is written in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, so nothing is borrowed from a theme and nothing slows you down later.

  2. 02

    Built for phones first

    I design at phone width first, then let the layout grow. For most businesses, most visitors arrive on a phone, so that's where the site has to feel best.

  3. 03

    Fast by default

    Projects average about 1.2 seconds to load: images sized for each screen, no heavy plugins, and nothing on the page that the page doesn't use.

  4. 04

    Accessible and findable

    Semantic structure, keyboard access, readable contrast, clean metadata and structured data are part of the build, not an extra you remember at launch.

Why don't you name your clients?

Because their businesses are theirs to talk about. Many clients prefer not to advertise who built their site, so my captions describe the kind of business and what I built, and leave names out unless a client has agreed. If you'd like to see work closer to your own project, ask me and I'll point you to the most relevant examples I can share.

Are the concept studies real projects?

They're real design work, but not client work. Atelier Studio, Lume and Meridian are self-initiated studies: I set a question, like how an architecture studio should feel online, and answered it in type, image and motion, using concept imagery and sample data. They show my thinking without a client's constraints.

Do all your projects look like this site?

No. This site is my own playground, with its glazed ceramic Z and tactile motion. Client sites look like their brands, not like mine. What carries over is the approach: designed from scratch, built mobile first, hand-coded and tested on real screens.

Can you work in a visual style you don't show here?

Yes. Style follows the brand and the audience. Some projects call for quiet editorial restraint, others for bold color and motion. The discovery and design steps exist to find the right style for you, not to repeat mine.
